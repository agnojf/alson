# Project Status Report -- Definition

A **status report** is a document that provides information on the current status of the project. The report may include information on progress since the last report and forecasts for cost and schedule performance.

Source: PMBOK Guide 8th Ed., Sec 2.7 (Definitions), page 247

Work performance reports are the physical or electronic representations of work performance information compiled in project documents, which are intended to generate decisions, actions, or awareness. They are circulated to project stakeholders through the communication processes defined in the project's communications management plan.

Examples: status reports and progress reports. May contain status of configuration items, earned value graphs and information, trend lines and forecasts, iteration burndown charts, defect histograms, contract performance information, and risk summaries.

Source: PMBOK Guide 8th Ed., Sec 2.7 (Definitions), pages 249-250

## Purpose

- Give stakeholders a scan-fast view of overall project health and the next critical item.
- Generate decisions and actions, not just awareness.
- Show accountability for progress, budget, and commitments.
- Surface risks, issues, assumptions, dependencies, and changes with owners.
- Record evidence for every health claim so readers can verify.

## Delivery Framing

Frame the report around these themes:

- **Header** -- project name, report date, overall RAG and trend, next critical date and item
- **Pulse** -- headline facts
- **Key metrics** -- current milestone, completion, target date, days to target
- **Health dashboard** -- RAG per dimension with trend, confidence, metric, finding, evidence
- **Decisions** -- decision, needed by, owner, priority
- **Progress** -- achievements and commitments
- **Milestones** -- name, baseline, forecast, variance, status, owner
- **Finance** -- summary rows and notes
- **Exceptions** -- risks, issues, assumptions, dependencies, changes
- **Actions** -- action, owner, due date, status
- **Evidence notes** -- limitations, confidence notes, sources

## Boundaries

A status report is:

- Evidence-driven. Every health claim traces to evidence.
- Scan-fast. Headings, tables, and labels make structure visible in seconds.
- Plain Markdown. It renders in any Markdown viewer.

A status report is NOT:

- A reconciliation of status updates into project documents (see project-statrep).
- A performance-domain review or project health review (see project-performance-domain-review).
- A daily priority plan (see most-important-tasks).
- A substitute for the project's live registers. It cites them instead of reproducing them.

## Evidence Rules

- Every RAG assignment cites specific evidence.
- Missing evidence produces Gray, not Green.
- Evidence must be current: within the reporting period or explicitly noted as "as of [date]".
- Forecasts and trends require at least two data points.
- Confidence is High (direct measurement), Medium (derived or estimated), or Low (assumption or guesstimate).
- A dimension with Low confidence evidence defaults to Amber unless confirmed Green by High confidence evidence.
