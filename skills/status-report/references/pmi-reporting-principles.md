# PMI Reporting Principles

These principles from the PMBOK Guide (8th Edition) guide the content and structure of the status report.

---

## Status Report Definition

A status report is a document that provides information on the current status of the project. The report may include information on progress since the last report and forecasts for cost and schedule performance.

Source: PMBOK Guide 8th Ed., Sec 2.7 (Definitions), page 247

## Work Performance Reports

Work performance reports are the physical or electronic representations of work performance information compiled in project documents, which are intended to generate decisions, actions, or awareness. The reports are circulated to project stakeholders through the communication processes as defined in the project's communications management plan.

Examples: status reports and progress reports. May contain status of configuration items, earned value graphs and information, trend lines and forecasts, iteration burndown charts, defect histograms, contract performance information, and risk summaries. The reports can be presented as dashboards, heat reports, stoplight charts, or other representations useful for promoting awareness and generating decisions and actions.

Source: PMBOK Guide 8th Ed., Sec 2.7 (Definitions), pages 249-250

## Monitor and Control Project Performance

This process involves tracking, reviewing, and reporting the overall project progress to meet performance objectives. It allows stakeholders to understand the current state of the project, recognize actions taken to address performance issues, and gain visibility into the future project status with cost and schedule forecasts.

Includes:
- Evaluating performance compared to plan
- Tracking utilization of resources, work completed, budget expended
- Demonstrating accountability
- Providing information to stakeholders
- Assessing whether deliverables are on track to deliver planned benefits
- Evaluating current risks, identifying new ones, and monitoring responses

Source: PMBOK Guide 8th Ed., Sec 2.1.6.7, pages 131-132

## Communications Management Plan

Describes how, when, and by whom information will be administered and disseminated. Includes:
- Stakeholder communication requirements
- Information to be communicated (language, format, content, level of detail)
- Reason for distribution
- Timeframe and frequency
- Person responsible
- Methods or technologies
- Guidelines and templates for status meetings and reports

Source: PMBOK Guide 8th Ed., Sec 2.7 (Definitions), pages 222-223

## Measurement Pitfalls to Avoid

- Hawthorne effect: measuring influences behavior
- Vanity metrics: show data but do not inform decisions
- Demoralization from unachievable goals
- Misusing metrics: focusing on wrong things
- Confirmation bias
- Correlation vs causation

Source: PMBOK Guide 8th Ed., Sec 2.1.6.7.1, page 133

## Stakeholder Communication

Consider who needs which information, level of detail, frequency, time zones, and communication media. There is rarely a one-size-fits-all approach.

Source: PMI Guide to Business Analysis, page 169
